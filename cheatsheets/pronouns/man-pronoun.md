# Pronomen "man" 🫥 — the pronoun for absolutely nobody in particular

"Man" = one / you / people in general / anybody. It's what you use when talking about *everyone and no one* at the same time. Very Swedish, honestly — consensus-flavored grammar.

---

## The meaning 💭

Use "man" when you mean **people in general**, not a specific he/she/they.

| Swedish | English | Vibe |
|---|---|---|
| Man får inte röka här | Nobody's allowed to smoke here | applies to literally everyone, including you 🚭 |
| Man får inte köra bil utan körkort | You may not drive without a license | universal rule, not a personal callout 🚗 |

---

## Declension — "man" is basically "han/hon" cosplaying as nobody 🎭

| Role | Form | Like... | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subject** (who does it) | **man** | han / hon | Man måste göra läxorna — *One must do one's homework* |
| **Object** (who it's done to) | **en** | honom / henne | Läraren hjälper en — *The teacher helps you/one* |
| **Possessive** (whose is it) | **ens** | hans / hennes | Ens barn är det viktigaste i ens liv — *One's child is the most important thing in one's life* |

### The parallel table 📊

| | Subject | Object | Possessive |
|---|---|---|---|
| he | han | honom | hans |
| she | hon | henne | hennes |
| **anybody** | **man** | **en** | **ens** |

Side-by-side proof it's the same pattern:

- Hans lärare hjälper honom. — *His teacher helps him.*
- Hennes lärare hjälper henne. — *Her teacher helps her.*
- Ens lärare hjälper en. — *One's teacher helps one.* 🤝

---

## Bonus plot twist: "du" moonlighting as "man" 🎭

When "du" is used generically (not talking to one specific listener), it quietly means the same thing as "man":

| Generic "du" | Real meaning |
|---|---|
| Om du vill lära dig svenska, måste du göra dina läxor | If one wants to learn Swedish, one must do one's homework |
| Om du gör allt som din lärare säger, blir du bra på svenska | If one does everything the teacher says, one will get good at Swedish |

So "du" isn't always "you, the person I'm looking at." Sometimes it's "du" cosplaying as "man" for a more casual/spoken register. 🎪

---

## The cheat-cheat 🧠

| Case | Form | Mnemonic |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | man | "man does the thing" |
| Object | en | "the thing happens to en... anyone" |
| Possessive | ens | "ens stuff, nobody's stuff in particular" |

Rule of thumb: if you'd say "one" or "people" or "you-in-general" in English → reach for **man/en/ens**, not a specific pronoun. 🫱
